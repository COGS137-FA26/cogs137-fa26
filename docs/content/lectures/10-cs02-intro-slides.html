Why It Matters

The most recent State of Global Air report (2025, data from 2023):

  • air pollution is the leading environmental risk factor for death worldwide
  • it contributed to 7.9 million deaths in 2023

Exposure is linked to asthma, lung disease, heart disease, diabetes, low birth weight, infections, dementia, and more.

Why We Need to Predict It

  • Monitors are sparse, and roughly 90% are in cities: an equity issue, since exposure in rural areas is poorly measured
  • Pollution can vary a lot even within the same city, and monitors aren’t necessarily near pollution sources
  • Health effects show up even at levels below guidelines, so we need fine spatial granularity to find who is at risk
  • Researchers have used machine learning with features like population density and road density to predict pollution where there are no monitors

So…we’re going to do the same

Question

With what accuracy can we predict US annual average air pollution concentrations?

This will specifically be at the granularity of zip code regional levels using predictors such as data about population density, urbanization, road density, as well as satellite pollution data and chemical modeling data.

Data

Outcome

value: the 2008 annual average PM2.5 (ug/m3) at each of 876 EPA gravimetric monitors, which capture fine particles on a filter that is then weighed

[image courtesy of Kirsten Koehler]

How do these values compare to health guidelines? Thresholds for annual average PM2.5 have become stricter over time:

When these data were collected (2008) Today
WHO guideline 10 ug/m3 (2005 guideline) 5 ug/m3 (2021 update)
EPA standard (primary NAAQS) 15 ug/m3 9 ug/m3 (2024 update)

We’ll come back to this during EDA: how many of our monitors exceed each threshold?

There are 48 features with values for each of the 876 monitors (observations).

The data comes from the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA), the US Census, and the National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS).
