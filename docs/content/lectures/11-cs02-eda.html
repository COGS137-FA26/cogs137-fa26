Q: I’m curious about some more of the ways people use gen AI to help them during and after their first draft.

A: I’m curious too for more thoughts on the lecture-specific disucssion! From what y’all said in lab 05 (summarized):

Creative ways to use GenAI:

  • Feedback on visualizations
  • AI as grader or critical reviewer (Catching duplicated work across teammates; jargon-only proofread; grader primed with your past feedback)
  • AI as a naive audience (Explain it back to me.)
  • Debugging and code cleanup (AI-generated edge cases to stress-test code, like how cutoff values would be handled)
  • Brainstorming analyses
  • Challenging conclusions: Alternative explanations, limitations, confounders, counterarguments.
  • Report structure and flow
  • Finding sources or background
  • explaining concepts like sensitivity and specificity (mock debate on sensitivity vs. specificity)
  • team planning and dividing tasks
  • and generating test data for code

Note: Telling AI to use Socratic mode

How to NOT use GenAI:

  • Having AI write the report, analysis or conclusions
  • Copying code they don’t understand
  • Made-up data, results or citations
  • Having AI do the whole project or a whole section
  • Letting AI make the decisions
  • Having AI build the visualizations from scratch
  • Having AI make the general-audience piece
  • Not disclosing AI use
  • Not uploading the CS01 data to an AI tool

Of 876 monitors, 28 (3%) were above the EPA standard in effect in 2008 (15 ug/m3). By today’s EPA standard (9 ug/m3), 686 (78%) would be. By WHO’s current guideline (5 ug/m3), 864 (99%).

A rough comparison: EPA’s official test uses a 3-year average, and these are 2008 values, not today’s air.

Feature Correlation

On Friday, you looked for variables that seemed redundant. Let’s check.

Why do we care if variables in our dataset are correlated?

  • we don’t want to include redundant variables
  • can add unnecessary noise to our algorithm causing a reduction in prediction accuracy
  • can cause our algorithm to be slower
  • can also make it difficult to interpret what variables are actually predictive

Which features track the outcome?

Guess: which feature is most correlated with value?

. . .

pm |>
  select(where(is.numeric)) |>
  cor() |>
  as_tibble(rownames = "feature") |>
  select(feature, r = value) |>
  filter(feature != "value") |>
  slice_max(abs(r), n = 10) |>
  ggplot(aes(x = r, y = fct_reorder(feature, r))) +
  geom_col(fill = "#2b6a99") +
  labs(x = "Correlation with value (r)", y = NULL, title = "Top 10 features") +
  theme_minimal()

. . .

  • CMAQ (the chemical model) is the strongest, followed by emissions (nei), roads (prisec), and the satellite measure (aod)
  • These are moderate correlations: no single feature predicts PM2.5 on its own

corrplot::corrplot(abs(PM_cor), order = "hclust", tl.cex = 0.5, col.lim = c(0, 1))

Observations:

  • development variables (imp), road density (pri), and the emission (nei) variables all seem to be correlated with their group
  • several predictors are moderately correlated with value (our outcome), but none strongly

Are the categories correlated with one another?

pm |>
select(log_nei_2008_pm25_sum_10000, popdens_county,
       log_pri_length_10000, imp_a10000, county_pop) |>
  GGally::ggpairs()

. . .

Reminder:

  • log_nei_2008_pm25_sum_10000 | Tons of emissions from major sources database (annual data) sum of all sources within a circle with a radius of 10000 meters of distance around the monitor (Natural log)
  • popdens_county | Population density (number of people per kilometer squared area of the county)
  • log_pri_length_10000 | Count of primary road length in meters in a circle with a radius of 10000 meters around the monitor (Natural log)
    – Highways only
  • imp_a10000 | Impervious surface measure
    – Within a circle with a radius of 10000 meters around the monitor
  • county_pop | Population of the county of the monitor

Log-transforming right-skewed data

pm |>
  mutate(log_popdens_county=log(popdens_county),
         log_pop_county = log(county_pop)) |>
  select(log_nei_2008_pm25_sum_10000, log_popdens_county,
       log_pri_length_10000, imp_a10000, log_pop_county) |>
